Technical Report XXXI

Probability and uncertainty estimation for a reference concentration threshold

T Chatzimichail, RA Chatzimichail, AT Hatjimihail

Abstract

Background: Background: Quantitative diagnostic probabilities depend on uncertain population distributions and imperfect measurements. We developed BDPU-Threshold in the Wolfram Language to estimate these probabilities from paired test and reference measurements. A separate simulation uses the parametric bootstrap to estimate sampling uncertainty. The notebooks include reference data, worked examples and optional analyses by age.

Results: NHANES 2013–2014 yielded 1,702 eligible adults aged 20–79 without a previous diabetes diagnosis, including 68 measured reference positives. Separate two- and three-dimensional mixtures were trained on the same 1,361 participants; each retained 6,000 posterior draws. At fasting plasma glucose 126 mg/dL, the age-free probability of analytical-error-free, same-visit reference positivity was 0.2228; parameter, new-result analytical measurement and total standard uncertainties were 0.0306, 0.0336 and 0.0455, with a 95% parameter credible interval of 0.1680–0.2860. At age 50 years, the age-conditioned probability was 0.1366 and total standard uncertainty 0.0395. On the same 341 holdout participants (14 positives), measured-reference Brier scores were 0.03044 without age and 0.03088 with age; the paired difference was 0.00044 (95% confidence interval −0.00198 to 0.00361). Mean predicted probabilities were 0.0295 and 0.0255, respectively, compared with an observed frequency of 4.11%; calibration intercepts were 0.42 and 0.59 and slopes 1.25 and 1.43. Age conditioning did not demonstrate improved calibration in this exploratory internal comparison.

Conclusions: BDPU-Threshold provides a Wolfram implementation of the defined probability and uncertainty calculations, with embedded reference posteriors and explicit computational contracts. The archived age-conditioned model showed greater underprediction in the observed holdout, with limited precision from 14 positives. The findings support a reproducible inferential workflow; they do not establish population-wide calibration, clinical benefit or validity of the latent reference endpoint.
