Technical Report XXXII

Latent class probabilities and uncertainty estimation without a gold standard

T Chatzimichail, RA Chatzimichail, AT Hatjimihail

Abstract

Background: When neither of two assays supplies a gold standard, an individual probability requires an explicit target. We developed the BDPU-LatentClass software estimating membership in the latent class with higher mean log concentration of assay 1. It separates uncertainty due to estimated model parameters from uncertainty due to analytical error in a new measurement.

Methods: We fitted a two-class Gaussian mixture to paired log fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) measurements from 1,361 NHANES participants. Class-specific covariance accommodates assay dependence. Prediction conditions on a new observed FPG value by weighting posterior draws by their predictive densities.

Results: At FPG 126 mg/dL, the probability of class 1 was 0.441 (95% parameter credible interval 0.204–0.684). The parameter, measurement and total standard uncertainties were 0.122, 0.082 and 0.147 in probability units. Replacing full class-specific covariance with an exploratory diagonal model raised the estimate to 0.666.

Conclusions: A Wolfram notebook connects an observation to both class probabilities, credible intervals and an uncertainty decomposition. Covariance assumptions materially affected the result. Interpreting the higher-FPG class as disease would require independent clinical evidence.
